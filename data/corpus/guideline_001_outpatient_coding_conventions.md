> SYNTHETIC DATA. This guideline paraphrases general coding principles for a technical exercise. It is not an official coding manual and must not be used for real coding decisions.

## 1. Purpose
This guideline summarizes internal conventions for outpatient diagnosis coding used by coders and by automated extraction tools.

## 2. Uncertain Diagnoses
2.1 In the outpatient setting, do not code diagnoses documented as "probable", "suspected", "questionable", "rule out", "possible", or "working diagnosis". Code instead the signs, symptoms, or abnormal findings documented for the encounter.
2.2 Example: "possible rheumatoid arthritis" documented at an outpatient visit is coded as the presenting joint symptoms (for example polyarthralgia), not as rheumatoid arthritis.
2.3 This convention differs from the inpatient setting, where uncertain diagnoses documented at the time of discharge may be coded as if established.

## 3. Specificity
3.1 Code to the highest level of specificity supported by the documentation. Unspecified codes are used only when the documentation does not support a more specific code.
3.2 Combination codes that link two conditions (for example diabetes with chronic kidney disease) require that the documentation establish the causal relationship, either explicitly ("diabetic CKD", "CKD due to diabetes") or through the ICD-10-CM "with" convention where it applies.

## 4. Historical and Resolved Conditions
4.1 Conditions documented as resolved, in remission with no active treatment, or "history of" are coded with personal history codes from categories Z85 through Z87.
4.2 Example: breast cancer treated in a prior year with no evidence of disease and no current treatment is coded Z85.3 (personal history of malignant neoplasm of breast), not as an active C50 malignancy.
4.3 A malignancy is coded as active only when the patient is receiving treatment directed at the malignancy or the documentation states that the disease is present.

## 5. Chronic Conditions
5.1 Chronic conditions may be coded at each encounter where they are documented and addressed. They are not coded from a problem list alone without evidence that the condition was addressed at the encounter.

## 6. Body Mass Index
6.1 BMI codes (category Z68) are reported only when an associated diagnosis such as obesity or overweight is documented by the provider.
